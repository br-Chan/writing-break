## How to Study Effectively for School or College

[:Video](https://youtu.be/CPxSzxylRCI?si=_IGqLFB-QuFOvdrt) by Memorize Academy
|                                   |     |
|-----------------------------------|-----|

This video was a good watch and very informative. Right off the bat it was stated that it was a collaboration between Memorize Academy and the "cognitive scientists" at [learningscientists.org](https://www.learningscientists.org/), so that transparency instantly puts it ahead of the game. The following is a brief summary of their top 6 study skills.

- **Spaced practice**: study at intervals over time instead of cramming near the deadline. Among other typical reasons why this is effective, the video also mentions how cashing in on revision now will reduce the load in the future, as you won't need to relearn the same information.
- **Interleaving**: switch between different ideas during study. This encourages your brain to form links between these ideas and understand how they relate to each other.
- **Elaboration**: explain and describe ideas to yourself in as much detail as you can. They also suggest to relate them to examples in you day-to-day life.
- **Concrete examples**: the brain is better at handling concrete ideas than abstract ones, so relate abstract information to [:a real-life example](#x-personal-note).
- **Dual coding**: combine words and visuals to more easily understand in the present and remember in the future.
- **Retrieval practice**: the one that they most highly recommend and that I most strongly agree with. Sit down and recall everything you know about the topic you're studying: the 2 different options suggested are vomiting all your knowledge onto a blank sheet of paper, and doing past tests. It's why so many students vie for those sweet, juicy past test papers (with their solutions).

I can't possibly transcribe the entire video, but I would highly recommend giving it a watch. They give proper reasonings for each of the 6 strategies, which is a welcome relief. They also have free posters:

[:Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn) is one of my favourite Classical composers. Not only did he make some great music and was basically a celebrity while he was alive, but he was also very good friends with Mozart and a tutor to Beethoven. It's interesting how the latter 2 became far more famous in the modern day than Hadyn did, but them's the breaks.

However, this sonata was a little underwhelming. The sonata is divided into 3 movements; the first was quick and lively, and my favourite; the second was too slow for my liking, and the final movement didn't hook me in like the first movement did  (although upon rewatch I admit it did have a nice motif). I would recommend listening to any of Haydn's more famous compositions such as the [:Surprise Symphony](https://youtu.be/tF5kr251BRs?si=wOY5AlPslWwKH4FT&t=15) (a truly *relaxing* piece, trust me) or the [:piece that would eventually become the German national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gott_erhalte_Franz_den_Kaiser).

I might find myself enjoying it more upon rewatches, but personally it just wasn't anything special.

## How to Make Yourself Study When You Have ZERO Motivation

[:Video](https://youtu.be/9oWOsocN7qg?si=nmYv8h3YkP3h4tF9) by Thomas Frank
|                                   |     |
|-----------------------------------|-----|
| **Upload date**                   | 16/3/18 |
| **Remember watching before?**     | No |
| **Should have watched sooner?**   | Yes |
| **Should you watch?**             | Yes |

And now we jump to 2018. In this video, the guy describes his "4-step process" that he goes through every time that he feels unmotivated.

**Exercise**: he demonstrated some scientific evidence behind this suggestion, which again was a nice respite from the knackering of 5-Minute Crafts. He suggests going out for a walk, but it appears any exercise is beneficial at a cognitive level.

**Commit to a single task**: he makes a wacky hamster ball analogy that surprisingly works really well.

**Clear to neutral**: another really great suggestion. Clean up your room or working space to a state such that you can fully focus on the task at hand.

**Low effort hack**: this suggestion amounts to avoiding "a blank piece of paper" at all costs. If you think you have any barrier to entry, forget about that barrier and just do something, even if it sucks. I hear this recommendation made across writing, coding and studying.

The rest of the video is just promotional material and a Skillshare sponsorship. But overall, a really great watch and it reaffirms the kinds of things I should be doing to avoid procrastination.
